Reaction & Commenting Language Bank

Reference Material (Unit 1)

Use these phrases to construct your responses in the analysis section or during class discussions.

Category Useful Phrases
1) Showing Agreement
  • I agree with your point that…
  • That’s a strong point.
  • I think you’re right because…
  • I completely agree, especially about…
  • That makes sense to me.
  • I see it the same way.
  • I like that idea because…
  • I agree to some extent.
  • That’s a convincing argument.
  • Your example really supports your point.
2) Partly Agreeing
(Balanced response)
  • I agree up to a point, but…
  • I see what you mean; however…
  • That’s true in some cases, but…
  • I partly agree, although…
  • I understand your point, but I’d add that…
  • I agree with the main idea, but the downside is…
  • That’s a fair point, but it depends on…
  • I’m with you on…, but I’m not sure about…
3) Disagreeing Politely
  • I’m not sure I agree with that.
  • I see your point, but I disagree because…
  • I’m afraid I disagree.
  • I don’t think that’s always true.
  • I would challenge that assumption.
  • I’m not convinced that…
  • I think the problem with that idea is…
  • From my perspective, that may not work because…
  • I respectfully disagree.
  • I get what you’re saying, but I see it differently.
4) Asking for clarification / Challenging gently
  • Could you clarify what you mean by…?
  • What do you mean exactly when you say…?
  • Could you give an example of…?
  • Why do you think that happens?
  • How does that connect to… (belonging/mastery/etc.)?
  • What evidence supports that idea?
  • Can you explain that in another way?
  • I’m not sure I understand—do you mean…?
  • What makes you say that?
  • How would that work in practice?
5) Adding ideas / Building on a point
  • I’d like to add that…
  • Another way to look at it is…
  • Building on your idea,…
  • That reminds me of…
  • We could also consider…
  • It might also help to…
  • A related point is…
  • One more factor could be…
  • What I find interesting is…
  • This connects to… (MI / principles) because…
6) Suggesting improvements
(Constructive feedback)
  • One suggestion would be to…
  • It might be worth + -ing…
  • You could strengthen your argument by…
  • You might want to include…
  • I’d recommend + -ing…
  • A clearer example could be…
  • It would be more convincing if you…
  • You could make this more specific by…
  • Have you considered + -ing…?
  • A practical next step would be to…
7) Evaluating feasibility
(Realistic vs Ideal)
  • That sounds realistic because…
  • That might be difficult in practice because…
  • That could work, provided that…
  • That would only work if/unless…
  • The risk is that…
  • The main challenge would be…
  • That depends on…
  • It’s a good idea, but it requires…
  • This might be easier to implement if…
  • This could be a long-term solution rather than an immediate one.
8) Using conditional-style reactions
  • That could work, provided that…
  • I’d agree with that as long as…
  • I’m not fully convinced—unless…
  • Unless we consider…, the idea may not…
  • Were the class to…, it would…
  • Had the teacher…, the result might have…
  • Without…, students wouldn’t…
  • It would be more effective if…
  • If we wanted real change, we’d have to…
  • That would have been different if…
